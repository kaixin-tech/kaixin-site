What Is Rice Husk Tableware? The Complete 2026 Buyer's Guide

Every year the world produces around 120 million tonnes of rice husk as a byproduct of milling — most of it burned or dumped. Rice husk tableware gives that waste a second life as safe, beautiful, reusable dinnerware. Here is everything an importer or brand needs to know.

What Exactly Is Rice Husk Tableware?

Rice husk (the hard outer layer of the rice grain) is ground into a fine powder and blended with a small amount of food-grade starch binder, then molded under high heat and pressure into plates, bowls, cups, boards, lunch boxes and kids sets. The result is a dense, smooth, wood-like material that is:

  • ✓Plant-based — made from agricultural waste, not petroleum
  • ✓BPA-free & plastic-free — no toxic leaching into food
  • ✓Durable — drop-resistant and microwave-safe for daily use
  • ✓Biodegradable — breaks down in soil at end of life

Why Buyers Are Switching to It

For distributors and private-label brands, rice husk dinnerware solves three problems at once: it meets rising consumer demand for sustainable products, it avoids the regulatory risk of plastic, and it tells a compelling "from farm waste to table" story that sells.

Our own collection spans 10 product lines — from chopping boards and serving trays to kids sets and lunch boxes — all manufactured in our 5,000 m² facility and shipped to 40+ countries.

What to Check Before You Source

Not all "eco" tableware is equal. When evaluating a supplier, ask for:

  • ✓Certifications — SGS, LFGB, FDA, REACH, BPA-Free, Melamine-Free
  • ✓Material ratio — true rice husk fiber, not filled with plastic
  • ✓OEM/ODM flexibility — custom color, logo and packaging
  • ✓Factory audit — visit or request a video tour

We publish our full certification set on the About Us page, and welcome video factory tours for serious buyers.

Is It Right for Your Market?

Rice husk tableware performs especially well in markets with strong green-consumer trends — Western Europe, North America, Australia, Japan and parts of Southeast Asia. It is a natural fit for gift shops, zero-waste stores, baby/parenting brands, and hospitality that wants a sustainable image.
