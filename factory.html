Process

From Husk to Carton

Six stages. Each one is on this page because it changes something a buyer can see or measure in the finished product.

1 · Husk intake

Rice husk arrives from mills in the rice-growing northeast and is checked for stone, sand and moisture. Wet husk is the fastest way to a brittle batch, so it is rejected before milling rather than dried and hoped for.

2 · Milling

Husk is ground to a consistent fiber. Particle size decides how the surface reads after pressing — coarse fiber gives the visible speckle most buyers recognize as plant fiber.

3 · Mixing

Fiber is blended with a food-grade starch binder. The ratio is weighed per batch; it is the single variable that most affects strength, weight and how the piece behaves in service — the material guide explains what that means for temperature and washing.

4 · Pressing

High temperature and pressure form the piece in one cycle on one of the 20+ presses. Temperature and dwell time are set per SKU and logged, not adjusted by feel between runs.

5 · Finishing

Trimming, edge sanding and surface inspection. This is where a rim becomes comfortable to drink from and where flash gets caught before it reaches a carton.

6 · QC & packing

Weight and dimension checks against the approved sample, drop and wash trials on the run, then packing with count, barcode and carton-drop verification, and loading photos before the container closes.

Why Rice Husk

A Byproduct, Not a Crop

Rice husk is the shell left after milling. It is not grown for this purpose, it does not compete with food for land, and the alternative uses are limited — historically it was burned or left to rot. Pressing it into tableware keeps the material in service for years instead of one season.

We are specific about what that does and does not mean. It means the feedstock is a byproduct. It does not, on its own, make a product certified compostable, and we do not label it that way. What we will say is that the material is SGS tested for food contact, contains no melamine or BPA, and is built to be washed and reused rather than thrown away after one meal.

Capacity

How We Quote a Delivery Date

Anyone can promise a number. These are the four variables that actually move it.

VariableWhat it does to the date
Press allocation20+ presses are shared across the range. A large single-SKU order can hold one press for days; a mixed order spreads across several and moves faster per item.
Color changesEvery color change costs setup time. Four colors in one order is not four times one color — but it is not free either, and the quotation says so.
New toolingA new mold adds drawing, tooling and trial pressing before production can start. We quote tooling separately so you can compare it against adapting an existing shape.
SeasonQ3 and Q4 are the busiest months because our customers are stocking for Christmas and the southern-hemisphere summer. Orders placed in that window take longer than the same order in February.

Chinese New Year is the other date to plan around. The plant slows for roughly two weeks around the holiday, and the weeks before it are the busiest of the year. For anything that must land in Q1 or early Q2, book production before mid-January. We would rather tell you this in October than explain it in December.

On Site

Visiting, and Sending Someone in Your Place

Come yourself

Fly into Shenyang Taoxian International Airport, or take the high-speed train from Beijing in about two and a half hours. Send dates in advance: the useful visit is a line walk-through, a look at the QC records and a sample review at the table, not a meeting room and a brochure. We will also show you the items we would not quote you for, which is usually the most informative part.

Or send an inspector

Appoint SGS, BV, TÜV, Intertek or a local agent. Give us three working days' notice, send the checklist and the AQL level, and we will have the lot staged when they arrive. The door, the batch records and the loading bay are open to whoever you send, so the report describes the plant you are actually buying from rather than a showroom.
